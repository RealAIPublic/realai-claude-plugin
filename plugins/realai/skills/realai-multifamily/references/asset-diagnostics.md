# Building context and operating diagnostics

Use the parts relevant to the user's question. An address or building name is enough to
begin an overview; ownership, a business plan, and an uploaded rent roll are not prerequisites.
A property overview, one expense comparison, and a broad operating review need different
amounts of evidence. Do not run a complete diagnostic for every property question.

## Building overviews and property searches

For an overview, establish identity, location, size, vintage, building style, and the
available rent or occupancy context that helps explain this building. Add ownership,
management, physical attributes, or nearby market conditions when relevant. Distinguish
observed property facts from modeled estimates and geographic context.

For a property-population search, translate the user's criteria into supported filters
such as location, unit count, vintage, and building style. Clarify only a consequential
ambiguity; apply supplied criteria before introducing preferences. Return matching
candidates with the evidence that makes each relevant. Do not imply a limited result set
exhausts the market. Ranking, coverage, ties, and comparability follow the shared
multi-entity guidance linked by the parent skill. An investment screen or acquisition
recommendation belongs to realai-underwriting; finding buildings alone does not.

## Expense comparisons

Area P&L benchmarks provide a first comparison, while a matched property set supports a
more specific operating judgment. For like-for-like peers, begin with the same county,
vintage within about ten years, unit count roughly 50–200% of the subject, and comparable
building style. These are starting criteria, not universal definitions of a peer.
If fewer than five peers have usable financials, consider market-level expansion and
explain the reduced comparability. State the relevant sample and filters with the result.

Compare major expense lines as a share of EGI and, where useful, dollars per unit or SF.
Compute peer medians and upper quartiles using consistent periods, definitions, and
available observations. A high expense-to-EGI ratio can reflect weak revenue as well as
high costs. Do not infer a historical expense trend from a single-period snapshot.

Separate items management can influence directly from structural pressures, while
recognizing that both may have controllable components. Staffing, repair practices,
advertising, and fees deserve different questions from insurance exposure, tax assessments,
and utility infrastructure. Do not classify the unexplained residual as manager failure.

- Verify management fees against the agreement's fee base, minimums, incentives, and scope.
  A dollar gap alone cannot show overpayment; broad fee norms need local support.
- Examine flood exposure, roof condition, construction, coverage limits, deductibles,
  and renewal timing before explaining insurance differences. Two risk factors do not
  establish the cause or amount of a premium gap.
- Keep P&L taxes distinct from assessed values and tax records. A potential appeal needs
  local assessment rules and comparable evidence, not a tax-to-EGI threshold alone.
- Low repairs or payroll may reflect efficiency, deferred work, or service reductions.
  Use maintenance, turnover, staffing, and resident outcomes to distinguish them.

## Rent upside and capture

Keep asking, in-place, achieved, and concession-adjusted rents distinct. Comp-supported
current market rent is stronger evidence of achievable rent than the subject's asking
rent alone. Compare equivalent unit types and finish levels before aggregating.

For comparable occupied units, calculate in code:

- Loss-to-lease: `(asking − in-place) / asking`; disclose the denominator and weights.
- Current market gap: `(supported market − in-place) / supported market`.
- Annualized gross rent gap: the sum of `(market − in-place) × 12` across included units.

Aggregate dollar rents before computing a portfolio ratio, or explain alternative weights.
Retain negative gaps. A present rent gap is neither guaranteed revenue nor a forecast.
Actual lease-end dates support capture timing; do not silently assume every lease lasts
12 months. Without those dates, keep timing conditional or show a labeled assumption.
Consider achieved tradeouts, concessions, turnover costs, downtime, renewal decisions,
and renovation completion. Separate renovation premiums from existing-condition upside.
The operating engine owns any resulting NOI scenario; a blanket expense pass-through
percentage cannot establish the NOI impact.

## Resident evidence and attribution

Use relevant income, credit, retention, and collections evidence to investigate operating
pressure. Aggregate resident profiles do not establish individual behavior or causation.
A credit score, lifestyle index, or rent burden alone cannot explain repair costs,
delinquency, or manager performance. Validate a proposed relationship against operating
records and distinguish an association from a hypothesis.

Rent-to-income and income-to-rent are inverses; keep monthly and annual periods aligned.
A ratio of median rent to median income is an aggregate indicator, not the distribution
of actual household rent burdens. Show distribution or sample limitations only when they
affect the interpretation being made.

For a manager question use the directly linked PM guidance. Numerical forecasts and
NOI/valuation scenarios use the engines linked by the parent skill. Variance bars, rent
positioning, or a trend chart can clarify the finding; choose the evidence and visual
that answer the question instead of filling report sections.
