# Assessing property management

Use the question to choose the assessment: a fee check, a leasing concern, or a decision
about retaining the manager. A question about management does not automatically require
a grade, a replacement recommendation, or a complete property study.

## Establish responsibility and the comparison

Identify the manager, responsibilities, and relevant tenure from reliable records. A
management-company identity does not establish the start date; a property sale does not
prove a management change. Use the agreement or documented transition when timing matters.
If tenure is unresolved, limit claims about changes under this manager.

Reuse suitable operating comparisons already established. Match peers on relevant size,
vintage, building style, positioning, geography, period, and metric definition. Explain
material differences and thin samples. Use the asset-diagnostics guidance for benchmark
construction, accessed through the parent skill.

Choose dimensions relevant to the concern: occupancy, leasing execution, retention,
comparable rent per SF, expense efficiency, service response, or contractual obligations.
Do not collect every metric merely to populate a scorecard.

## Attribution requires evidence

Separate what is observed from possible explanations. Consider market movement, physical
condition, owner-approved spending or pricing constraints, unit mix, concession policy,
and operational decisions. A residual gap after several comparisons is still unexplained
unless evidence supports assigning it to management.

- Compare changes over the same period, including the subject's starting position. A
  current occupancy gap minus a market occupancy change is not a causal estimate.
- Match bedroom mix and area definitions for rent-per-SF comparisons. Strong rent can
  coexist with avoidable vacancy, and high occupancy can reflect excessive discounting.
- Distinguish structural costs from decisions the manager controls. Evaluate staffing
  and repairs together when outsourcing or deferred work shifts costs between lines.
- Use actual renewals, move-outs, service records, and lease terms to investigate retention.
  A public review theme can support a question; it does not establish the cause or cost.
- Aggregate resident credit, income, or demographic characteristics do not prove that
  residents caused operating costs or excuse management performance. Do not assign a
  dollar cost to a credit-score threshold or infer individual behavior from area data.

Where longitudinal evidence permits, compare the subject with similar properties over
the manager's documented tenure, accounting for renovations and material changes. Label
remaining attribution uncertainty. Do not claim a controlled comparison when only a
cross-sectional snapshot is available.

## Metrics and contract checks

Compute comparisons in code with consistent units and periods. Percentage-point changes
and percentage changes answer different questions. Distinguish listing-to-signing time,
physical vacancy days, and turnover-ready time; they are not interchangeable proxies.
Retention denominators and observation windows must match before comparing rates.

For management fees, reconcile contractual base, minimums, scope, incentives, leasing
fees, and reimbursements to actual charges. Fees divided by EGI can support comparison,
but the contract may define a different fee base. Broad size-based fee ranges are context
only when supported by current comparable evidence; crossing a generic threshold cannot
establish overpayment or justify replacement.

If the user requests an improvement scenario, explain the operational assumptions and
cost of achieving it. The operating engine owns NOI and valuation calculations. Closing
a peer gap is an illustrative scenario, not measured manager-created value. Include
implementation costs and potential service or occupancy effects when material.

## Recommendations proportional to the finding

For a supported operating issue, identify a practical action, a measurable outcome, and
a review period grounded in lease timing or execution needs. Avoid universal 90-day
plans, arbitrary weighted grades, or a mandatory retain/improve/replace vocabulary.
If a scored comparison is requested, explain the criteria and weight choices and do
not treat unobserved dimensions as failures.

For a replacement decision, consider termination rights, notice, fees, transition risk,
staff continuity, and realistic alternatives. Use sourced information for other operators'
capabilities and fees. Similar complaints across a firm's properties may warrant further
review; selection bias and different asset conditions limit the conclusion.

A peer-comparison chart or a dated performance trend can make the evidence clearer.
State consequential uncertainty next to the affected judgment; omit unavailable metrics
that do not affect the user's management question.
