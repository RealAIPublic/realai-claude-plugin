# Unit rent and renewal decisions

Use this guidance for asking rents, renewal terms, or an operating pricing plan. Work on
the units and decision the user named; pricing one vacancy does not require pricing the
entire property. A usable in-chat recommendation or table is sufficient unless a tracker
is requested.

## Evidence needed for the decision

Reuse the property and constraints already supplied. Current unit records establish the
unit's rent, occupancy, condition, and lease terms; comparable evidence supports a target.
No particular vendor's report is required. If reliable unit facts are unavailable, give
the supported property-level context and explain which fact prevents the unit decision.
Ask for relevant records only when they materially improve or unblock that decision.

Establish the report's as-of date and property identity before combining records. Use the
shared reconciliation guidance for conflicting evidence. An uploaded report is not
automatically current, and a later listing observation does not automatically establish
physical vacancy. The report's market-rent column is the operator's opinion, to be tested.

For detailed report mapping, calculation definitions, or a requested tracker, use the
separate references linked directly from the parent skill. Load only what is needed.

## Support achievable rent

Compare equivalent beds, baths, size, finish, and lease terms. Account for concessions
consistently: asking, achieved, in-place, and net-effective rents are different measures.
Use public competitor listings and authorized subject-property records; do not solicit
nonpublic competitor pricing or propose coordination among operators.

A comp-supported range is an estimate of positioning, not a guarantee of execution.
Use recent subject leasing outcomes, vacancy duration, renewal acceptance, and leasing
funnel records to assess whether that positioning is plausible. Public reviews may
suggest service issues to investigate; ratings alone cannot quantify a rent discount.
Do not promise that a price cut will lease a unit within a particular period.

Aggregate resident income or credit evidence may inform property-level uncertainty.
Do not attribute a building's average credit or income profile to a specific resident,
or use it to infer that resident's willingness to pay. Renewal judgments should rest on
actual lease terms, consistent policies, permitted considerations, and supported economics.

## Timing and constraints

Use actual lease-end dates and applicable notice requirements when scheduling offers or
marketing. Do not insert a default 60-day notice period or inferred 12-month lease as
fact. If dates are missing, separate a rent recommendation from conditional timing.
Assess observed seasonal patterns only from suitable history; one snapshot cannot reveal
a leasing season or establish a future trajectory.

Before presenting an actionable renewal increase, check applicable state and local rules,
property exemptions, restrictions, and current effective dates using authoritative public
sources. A prior year's CPI-indexed cap is not a current cap. If applicability or the
current limit remains unresolved, make the affected recommendation conditional and explain
the specific unresolved point. Do not withhold unrelated market analysis.

Check renovation scope and completion dates when a premium depends on upgrades. Separate
as-is market gaps from renovation premiums. Units with disputed status, material payment
exceptions, or missing lease terms may need review before a dated renewal action; do not
convert an accounting flag into an automatic nonrenewal recommendation.

## Choose a supported action

Consider vacancy cost and retention economics together with price. Weak conversion or
slow leasing can reflect exposure, condition, screening delays, seasonality, or price.
A rent reduction is one possible response; evidence should support why it is appropriate.
If the user's preferred rent push conflicts with observed execution, explain the
tradeoff and offer a supported scenario instead of silently overriding the objective.

For a review cadence or staged adjustment, state the conditions that trigger another
review, such as no qualified activity by a specified date. Select intervals from actual
leasing conditions and the user's constraints; avoid a universal cut schedule or an
assumed rent floor.

Run derived metrics in code. The parent skill's operating engine owns NOI consequences;
its forecasting engine owns numerical forecasts or confidence bands when requested.
Neither engine is required merely to compare current unit rents with current comps.
A rent-position chart or lease-expiration chart is useful when its evidence clarifies
the decision. Keep irrelevant unavailable fields out of the answer.
