# Reading a market for a strategy

Use this guidance when the user's purpose changes how the same evidence should be read.
It supplies analytical priorities, not a scorecard or a required set of queries. Topic
names are catalog hints; the live schema supplies available fields and definitions.

| Strategy | Evidence that matters most | Main tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Stabilized MF acquisition | Achieved rent growth, occupancy, competing supply, migration and income trajectory; market cap rates when pricing matters | Durable occupancy can come at a price that leaves little yield cushion |
| MF value-add | Asking versus in-place spread supported by tradeouts, comparable renovated rents, household resources, competing deliveries | A geographic spread suggests a hypothesis; it does not establish achievable renovation premiums at a building |
| MF ground-up | Asking rents for comparable new product, absorption, under-construction units, employment and household formation | Current strength may coincide with a delivery wave during lease-up |
| SFR aggregation | SFR rents by unit category, home values relative to income, suitable housing stock, rent-to-value economics, SF permits | Home-price appreciation can outpace rents and reduce yield; HOA or regulatory constraints need property-specific evidence |
| BTR development | SFR rent trajectory, household composition, competing rental product, land and construction assumptions when supplied | SF permits include owner-occupied housing and do not measure BTR competition on their own |
| Mixed or opportunistic | The evidence distinguishing plausible MF and SFR approaches | Explain which strategy each place supports rather than collapsing incompatible strategies into one generic score |

Useful topic families include MF rent and occupancy, SFR rents, `home_sales_and_values`,
`home_personas`, `supply_snapshot`, `permit_ts`, `caprate_ts`, migration, employment,
and household financials. Choose only those needed for the live question.

For comparisons, apply the same strategy and period across places. A sensitivity view
can show how changing priorities changes the preferred market. Use a composite only
when it helps the requested decision and follow the shared scoring discipline.

Market fit is not a property acquisition recommendation. Asset-level rents, costs,
financing, and business-plan assumptions belong to transaction underwriting when the
user moves from selecting a market to evaluating a deal.
