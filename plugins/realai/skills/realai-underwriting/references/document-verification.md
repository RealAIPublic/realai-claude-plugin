# Checking deal documents and claims

Use for the documents, figures, or claims the user wants checked. A narrow reconciliation
needs a narrow answer; a broader diligence request can examine the package. Neither
requires a standard verdict, mandatory valuation, or a new acquisition study.

## Define what can be checked

Identify the relevant document, date, period, subject, and claim. Use supplied files,
quoted passages, or other accessible source material. If the requested document is not
available, ask for that document or passage; continue any independent, supported checks.
Do not claim to have verified material that was not inspected.

Treat a broker projection as a claim to test, not as established fact or automatic error.
A recent operating report can also contain mistakes. Use the reconciliation reference
for source conflicts; definitions, recency, and fitness matter more than source labels.
Compare actuals with actuals and identify when a difference is a planned future change.

## Match the check to the evidence

| Material under review | Useful checks |
|---|---|
| T12 or operating statement | Internal totals, reporting periods, accounting basis, recurring versus nonrecurring items, concessions, reimbursements, taxes, and expense classification. |
| Rent roll | Occupied versus leased units, contracted versus collected rent, concessions, vacancy, unit mix, arrears if supplied, and lease expiration concentrations. |
| Offering memorandum | Reconcile income and costs to supporting records; test rent, growth, occupancy, renovation, and pricing claims against relevant evidence. |
| Rent or sale comps | Verify the quoted transaction or rent, terms, date, product, size, and condition; investigate material omitted alternatives and selection bias. |
| Development budget | Check scope, overlapping allowances, omitted carry or lease-up costs, cost basis, timing, contingency, and support for incentives. |
| Appraisal or specialist report | Check effective date, scope, assumptions, exclusions, and whether the cited conclusion actually answers the claim. |
| Supplied financial-model figures | Compare accessible inputs and reported outputs, dates, and assumptions. Value-only extracts do not verify formulas or the workbook as a whole. |

For multifamily, compare the relevant operating measures with like-for-like benchmarks.
RealAI financials can be modeled annual estimates; never relabel them as a verified T12.
A benchmark difference is a reason to investigate, not proof that the property's actuals
are wrong. Unit-count or year-built conflicts can matter; resolve definitions and dates
rather than trusting one source unconditionally.

For other commercial assets, rely on documents and public evidence for building
operations. Review remaining lease term, tenant concentration, rollover, downtime,
reimbursements, and tenant-improvement/leasing costs where those concepts apply. Do not
pretend the MCP supplies commercial property operations or apply multifamily norms to
hotel, office, industrial, or other operating structures.

For a lease-up, distinguish temporary concessions and vacancy from stabilized assumptions.
Compare achieved rents and absorption with the plan and inspect reserves and carrying
costs when provided. A short lease or temporary loss may be expected during lease-up;
the question is whether the timing and economics still support the plan.

## Materiality and uncertainty

Assess dollar impact, percentage change, decision sensitivity, source quality, and the
nature of the item together. A small cap-rate, coverage, or occupancy change can matter
more than a large change in a minor expense. Do not use universal percentage bands or
an invented coverage cutoff to decide whether a discrepancy exists.

Thin samples and stale evidence qualify a comparison. They neither establish an error
nor require hiding a consequential uncertainty. NULL fields do not prove suppression or
weak operations. Omit irrelevant unavailable benchmarks; disclose what prevents a
requested verification or changes the conclusion.

State the direction and basis of a difference. Distinguish document-stated, calculated,
adjusted, and benchmark values. An independently corroborated figure still retains its
source and date; a calculation is not independent verification of the inputs.

Public incidents, reviews, environmental context, or area statistics belong only when
they bear on the question. Separate property-specific evidence from area-wide patterns,
check recency and corroboration, and consider relevant contrary evidence. An allegation
or recurring review theme is not a proven operational fact.

## Quantify only the requested implications

Use the reconciled source figures and follow
`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/shared/output-conventions.md` for derived arithmetic. A calculation
does not verify its inputs. Limit claims to accessible evidence; do not claim a workbook
formula audit, recalculation, or modification from a document excerpt or value-only table.

Recompute value or debt coverage when the user asks for that implication or it is needed
to explain a material finding. Use applicable financing terms and lender requirements;
do not invent an asking price, financing structure, or universal debt-coverage floor.
Absent financial evidence constrains a financial conclusion, not every other finding.

Lead with what the check establishes. A compact reconciliation table or variance chart
can make a material difference clear. Explain whether it affects price, needs further
verification, or changes the decision only where evidence supports that implication.
The absence of detected issues is not a clean bill of health for unreviewed material.
